# Viewpoint Transitions

A viewpoint switch is an editorial and continuity event, not a decorative tag.

## 1. Transition record

Every transition names:

- from and to shots;
- from and to viewpoint profiles;
- trigger;
- bridge type;
- whether camera ownership, focalization, or audition changes;
- knowledge effect;
- state event IDs when state also changes;
- continuity requirements.

## 2. Supported bridges

- motivated hard cut;
- eyeline cut;
- match on action;
- object match;
- sound bridge;
- occlusion bridge;
- blink bridge;
- light bridge;
- registered continuation;
- re-establishing cut.

## 3. External to first-person

Use a shared anchor such as gaze target, raised object, hand motion, sound source, or occlusion. Preserve prop ownership, subject position, light direction, and story state.

## 4. First-person to external reaction

The external shot must begin from the accepted end state of the first-person view. Preserve camera-owner posture, visible action, prop location, and the timing of the reaction.

## 5. Knowledge changes

A camera cut alone does not grant omniscience. If the next shot reveals hidden information, record the focalization change and story purpose. If no reveal is intended, the new camera must preserve concealment.

## 6. State changes

When action across the cut changes wardrobe, injury, prop ownership, or environment, name the state event and carry the pre-event or post-event snapshot consistently. Do not let the cut create an unexplained state change.

## 7. Transition dimensions

A viewpoint transition may change several dimensions independently:

```text
camera ownership
camera position
viewpoint profile
focal character
focalization scope
point of audition
shot scale
knowledge visibility
visible state projection
```

Do not describe the transition only as `third person to first person`. State which dimensions change and which continuity facts remain fixed.

## 8. External to over-the-shoulder

Use a gaze target, approach, shared object, or line of action as the bridge. Preserve:

- aligned character screen side;
- shoulder or head foreground ownership;
- facing subject position and eyeline;
- shared prop position;
- axis side;
- light direction;
- current state and wardrobe.

The over-the-shoulder shot should add relational pressure, contact information, or attention alignment rather than merely crop closer.

## 9. Over-the-shoulder to embodied first person

Use a shared anchor that can occupy both frames:

- raised object;
- offered hand;
- facing character's eyes;
- doorway or window;
- sound source;
- foreground occlusion.

The first-person opening must match the camera owner's actual position and facing from the prior external shot. Visible owner limbs must emerge from plausible frame edges and remain owned by that character.

## 10. Embodied first person to external

The external shot begins from the camera owner's accepted body state. Record:

- owner posture and balance;
- hand and prop state;
- head or gaze orientation;
- other character position;
- point of audition change;
- action phase at the cut.

An external reaction shot can show what the first-person shot could not, but it must not invent a different pose or prop owner.

## 11. Focal character switch

When an external scene changes alignment from C01 to C02, decide whether the cut changes only coverage or also focalization.

Coverage-only switch:

```text
The camera shows C02's reaction, but the scene remains restricted to what C01 knows.
```

Focalization switch:

```text
The scene now follows C02's attention and may reveal information already known to C02 but not C01, if the story approves that disclosure.
```

Record the knowledge effect explicitly.

## 12. Point of audition switch

Audio may become subjective without changing camera ownership:

- external camera with C01's ringing ears;
- objective picture with device microphone audio;
- first-person camera with objective room mix;
- external reaction shot carrying sound from the prior subjective insert.

Record the transition and review whether the audio perspective changes at the intended moment.

## 13. State transition across a viewpoint cut

A cut can coincide with a state change, but the state change needs a cause and event. Examples:

- a prop passes hands during match-on-action;
- a coat is removed behind an occlusion bridge;
- a time jump changes weather, wardrobe, or age;
- a reveal shows a wound that already existed;
- an impact creates a new injury.

Distinguish:

```text
state changed
state became visible
camera changed
knowledge changed
```

These may occur together but are not the same event.

## 14. Transition acceptance

Review the two shots and the cut for:

- spatial relation;
- axis and screen direction;
- eyeline or object match;
- action phase;
- prop ownership;
- state and wardrobe;
- light direction;
- focalization and knowledge;
- point of audition;
- emotional continuity;
- intended story function.

If the cut is unclear, repair the smallest failing dimension. Do not redesign the whole scene by default.
